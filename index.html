What you get

Built like a real app, not a wrapper.

Everything runs on the phone. There's no queue to wait in, no account to make, and nothing to pay for.

Every quality, not just 360p

720p, 1080p and above arrive as separate video and audio streams. The app fetches both and merges them with Android's own muxer — a stream copy, so nothing is re-encoded and nothing is lost.

Real MP3, with the cover art

No site actually serves MP3. StreamGarden decodes the audio and re-encodes it on your phone with a built-in LAME encoder, then writes the title, artist and thumbnail into the file.

Downloads at full speed

Servers throttle a single connection after its first burst. Each file comes down as several ranged chunks at once, so a long video finishes in a fraction of the time.

An offline music player inside

Every song on your phone, in one place — playlists, favourites, swipe gestures, and playback that keeps going with the screen off, complete with lock-screen and headset controls.

Share a link straight to it

Tap Share anywhere — YouTube, Instagram, your browser — and pick StreamGarden. It starts working the moment it opens. Copy a link instead and it offers to fetch that too.

Whole playlists, your pick

Paste a playlist and every video shows up with a checkbox. Tick the ones you actually want — nothing downloads until you say so.

Installing

It's an APK, so there's one extra step.

StreamGarden isn't on the Play Store, so Android asks your permission before installing it. This is normal for apps installed outside the store.

1 · Download the file

Tap the download button. Chrome may warn that this type of file can harm your device — that warning appears for every APK. Choose Download anyway.

2 · Open it and allow

Tap the downloaded file. If Android says installs from this source are blocked, tap Settings, turn the permission on, and go back. Then tap Install.

Nervous about that warning? Fair. The app is open source — you can read every line, or build it yourself and install your own copy. It asks for no camera, microphone, location or contacts permission, because it never needs them.

Under the hood

For anyone who wants to know how.

The interesting part isn't the interface — it's that all of this happens on a phone, with no server in the middle.

Extraction
Runs on the device with NewPipeExtractor and an embedded JavaScript engine that executes the site's own player code to solve the stream cipher. Your phone's IP does the talking, so it isn't blocked the way hosted downloaders are.
Merging
Android MediaMuxer, stream copy only — no re-encode, no generation loss.
MP3
Decoded with MediaCodec, re-encoded by a bundled LAME, tagged with ID3v2 (title, artist, cover art).
Transfer
Parallel ranged chunks over OkHttp, with resume, cancel and live progress.
Music player
A foreground service with MediaSession — background playback, notification, lock-screen, headset and Bluetooth controls.
Instagram & Facebook
Resolved through a Cobalt instance. Public ones are built in; point it at your own for reliability.
Built with
Capacitor 6 · React 19 · TypeScript · a custom native Java plugin

Questions

Straight answers.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

Google's Device and Network Abuse policy doesn't allow apps that download content from platforms like YouTube. That's why NewPipe, Seal and every similar app are distributed the same way — as an APK, outside the store.

Is it safe to install?

The full source is public, so nothing is hidden. It requests no camera, microphone, location or contacts access — check the permission list yourself before installing. The only network calls it makes are to the site you're downloading from.

Does it collect my data?

No accounts, no analytics, no ads. The app registers a device identifier so the developer can push update notices and, if ever needed, disable a build — nothing about you, your files or your activity is collected.

Where do my files go?

Into Downloads/StreamGarden on your phone's own storage — visible to every file manager, gallery and music player. They're ordinary files; the app doesn't lock them in.

How do updates work?

Come back here and install the newer APK over your current one — nothing is lost. The app also shows a notice inside when a new version is out.

Instagram or Facebook isn't downloading. Why?

Those platforms have no public API, so they're resolved through a shared Cobalt server that can be busy or rate-limited. Running your own takes about ten minutes and makes them reliable — the setup guide is in the repository.

Can I download things I don't own the rights to?

The app is a tool, and it doesn't decide that for you. Downloading copyrighted material can breach a platform's terms and your local law. Use it for your own content, for licensed and public-domain material, and for things you're allowed to keep offline.
